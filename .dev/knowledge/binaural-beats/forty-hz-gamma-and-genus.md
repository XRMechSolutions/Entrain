# The 40 Hz "Gamma" Claim: GENUS Is Not Binaural Beats

## The conflation to avoid

Binaural-beats products frequently cite the famous MIT 40 Hz Alzheimer's research
to borrow credibility. **That research does not use binaural beats**, and its
results do not transfer.

| | MIT "GENUS" (Tsai/Boyden lab) | 40 Hz binaural beat |
|---|---|---|
| Stimulus | **40 Hz light flicker** (strobing LEDs) + **40 Hz amplitude-modulated "clicking" sound**, presented identically to both ears | Two steady pure tones ~40 Hz apart, one per ear |
| Is 40 Hz physically present? | **Yes** — real pulsing energy | **No** — illusory, reconstructed in the brainstem |
| Cortical drive | Strong steady-state phase-locking | Weak/variable (see `does-entrainment-happen.md`) |
| Demonstrated effects | Reduced amyloid-β/tau and glymphatic clearance in mouse models; early human trials (Cognito "Spectris") | Auditory steady-state response only; **no** amyloid or Alzheimer's effect shown |

## Why it matters

"40 Hz" and "gamma" are shared vocabulary, so a 40 Hz *binaural* track can be
marketed as if MIT's dementia headlines validate it. They do not — different
stimulus, different mechanism, different (clinical) population. Physically-present
rhythmic stimulation (flicker, clicks, isochronic, monaural) is a genuinely
different and stronger driver than the binaural illusion.

There are **no binaural-beat studies** showing amyloid reduction, neuroprotection,
or Alzheimer's benefit. Any product implying otherwise is borrowing evidence that
does not apply to it.
