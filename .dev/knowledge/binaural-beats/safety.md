# Binaural Beats: Safety

## Seizure / epilepsy risk — the visual-vs-audio distinction

| Point | Detail | Grade |
|-------|--------|-------|
| Photosensitive epilepsy is **visual only** | Triggered by flashing/flickering light and high-contrast patterns, ~3–60 Hz (most provocative 16–25 Hz). Sound is not on epilepsy-trigger lists | Established (Epilepsy Society) |
| It does **not** transfer to audio | Auditory entrainment uses a different pathway (cochlea → brainstem → auditory cortex); the photoparoxysmal mechanism does not apply | Established by mechanism |
| Sound-triggered (audiogenic/musicogenic) seizures exist but are **very rare** | No documented evidence that binaural beats trigger seizures in the general population | Established (rarity); Insufficient (BB-specific) |

**Practical stance:** meaningful seizure risk from audio binaural beats for the
general public is not supported. As precautions: do not pair beats with visual
strobe/flicker; people with epilepsy (especially musicogenic) should consult a
clinician.

## Other cautions

| Caution | Basis |
|---------|-------|
| Don't use sleep/deep-relaxation (delta/theta) tracks while driving or operating machinery | These reduce arousal and can induce drowsiness (mechanism-based) |
| Protect hearing | Prolonged loud audio risks noise-induced hearing loss/tinnitus — moderate volume, take breaks |
| Timing | Alerting (beta/gamma) frequencies near bedtime may disrupt sleep; some report irritability |
| Sensitive listeners | Possible discomfort for people with hyperacusis or anxiety disorders |
| Long-term safety | Data are insufficient |
