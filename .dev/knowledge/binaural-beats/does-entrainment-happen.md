# Does Listening to a Binaural Beat Actually Shift Your Brainwaves?

## Short answer: mostly no (Contested, leans negative)

Two different phenomena get conflated:

| Phenomenon | What it is | Evidence |
|-----------|-----------|----------|
| ASSR / FFR (auditory steady-state / frequency-following response) | A small evoked response time-locked to the stimulus, in the auditory pathway | Established but narrow — recordable (e.g. 40 Hz; Schwarz & Taylor 2005; Draganova 2008) |
| Brainwave entrainment (BWE) | A broad, sustained rise in EEG band power that shifts the brain into a "state" | Contested — the majority of controlled studies are null |

A recordable ASSR proves the auditory system *tracks* the beat. It does **not**
prove the cortex has entered an "alpha" or "theta" state.

## What controlled EEG studies find

- **Systematic review (Ingendoh et al. 2023):** of 14 controlled EEG studies, only
  ~5–6 showed any entrainment-consistent result (scattered across theta/alpha/
  gamma), ~8 were null, 1 mixed. **No beta study ever showed entrainment.** Every
  study that embedded beats in pink noise was null. Conclusion: the question
  "cannot be settled," and entrainment is usually *assumed, not measured*.
- **López-Caballero & Escera 2017:** tested five beat frequencies (theta→gamma),
  binaural and acoustic, vs a pink-noise baseline — **no EEG power enhancement at
  any beat frequency**, and no heart-rate or skin-conductance change.
- A 2021 study found 40 Hz beats improved an attention measure **while showing no
  neural entrainment** — any behavioral effect was not via entrainment.

## Binaural is the *weakest* of the three (mechanistically grounded)

Because the binaural beat is physically absent and reconstructed subcortically, it
drives a **weaker cortical response than monaural beats or isochronic
(amplitude-modulated) tones**, whose rhythm is physically present in the envelope.
Schwarz & Taylor (2005): the binaural-beat ASSR was *smaller* than the
monaural-beat ASSR and vanished above ~3 kHz carrier. This directly contradicts the
common marketing claim that binaural beats are the most powerful entrainment
method — they appear to be the least.
