# Binaural Beats: Evidence on Claimed Effects

## Overall (modest, low-certainty)

Two quantitative meta-analyses anchor the field, both finding a **medium overall
effect undercut by heavy caveats**:

- Garcia-Argibay et al. (2019): 22 studies, 35 effect sizes; overall **Hedges'
  g = 0.45** across memory, attention, anxiety, pain.
- Basu & Banerjee (2023): 15 studies; overall **g = 0.40** for memory and attention.

Both stress effects are moderated by beat frequency, exposure duration, and timing
(exposure *before* a task often beats exposure *during*); their own systematic
reviews describe "conflicting results."

## By outcome

| Outcome | Evidence | Grade |
|---------|----------|-------|
| Anxiety (acute/procedural) | Strongest signal; perioperative meta-analysis SMD −1.38 vs blank audio — but I²≈92% (extreme heterogeneity), unblindable controls | Mixed, leaning positive |
| Memory / attention | Pooled g≈0.40; individual studies inconsistent, sometimes null/negative; theta-vs-beta effects don't replicate cleanly | Mixed |
| Creativity | Essentially one study; effect depended on individual dopamine markers | Weak / Insufficient |
| Mood | Contradictory POMS studies; not a primary outcome in either meta-analysis | Insufficient |
| Pain | Postoperative SMD −0.61 (I²≈76%); inconclusive vs no-treatment controls | Weak / Mixed |
| Sleep | 0.25 Hz beats shortened slow-wave latency in a nap study (N=12); insomnia trial was open-label, uncontrolled | Weak |
| Meditation / relaxation | Theta (~6 Hz) raised parasympathetic HRV (d≈0.59) in one review | Mixed-to-moderate |
| Focus (beta/gamma) | Modest and most variable (d≈0.34); several null sustained-attention results | Mixed / Weak |

## Why the evidence is weak

- **Small samples** (EEG studies often n≈4–47), student-heavy populations.
- **Blinding is near-impossible** — controls are blank audio, noise, or silence.
- **Extreme heterogeneity** — pooled I² of 75–95% in clinical meta-analyses;
  carriers, beats, durations, and outcome measures all differ.
- **High risk of bias**; publication bias rarely tested.
- **Mechanism unconfirmed** (see `does-entrainment-happen.md`) — most applied
  studies assume entrainment without measuring it.
- **A large counter-result:** Klichowski et al. (2023), a home study with
  **N≈1,000**, found binaural beats **worsened** complex-task performance
  regardless of frequency.

**Honest bottom line:** promising-but-not-established for acute anxiety; mixed for
cognition; weak/insufficient for mood, pain, creativity, and sleep. Real effects
exist in pooled data but are modest and dominated by low-quality studies.
