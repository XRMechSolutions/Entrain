# Binaural Beats: Auditory Mechanism & Perception

## Core mechanism (Established)

A binaural beat is a **neural illusion, not an acoustic event**. Two steady pure
tones with a small frequency difference are delivered separately — one to each ear
(e.g. 400 Hz left, 410 Hz right). No 10 Hz sound exists in the air or in either
earcup; the "beat" at the difference frequency is computed inside the auditory
pathway. This is why **headphones are mandatory**: the two tones must stay
channel-separated until the brain combines them.

Contrast with a *monaural* or *isochronic* beat, where the rhythm is physically
present in the sound's amplitude envelope and is audible on a loudspeaker.

## Where the beat is generated (Established)

The difference frequency first arises **subcortically in the superior olivary
complex (SOC)** — specifically the medial superior olive, the first nucleus to
receive input from both ears. It reuses the interaural-phase-difference machinery
the brain uses to localize sound. Phase-locked responses continue in the inferior
colliculus and travel up to auditory cortex (human MEG source localization:
Draganova 2008; reviews: Orozco Perez 2020, López-Caballero & Escera 2017).

Because the beat is reconstructed from subcortical phase-locking rather than
present in the signal, the downstream cortical response is **weak and variable** —
see `does-entrainment-happen.md`.

## Perceptual limits (Established)

| Parameter | Constraint | Why |
|-----------|-----------|-----|
| Carrier (base) frequency | Illusion only below ~1000 Hz; strongest ~250–500 Hz; undetectable above ~3000 Hz | Neural phase-locking fails at high frequencies (Licklider; Schwarz & Taylor 2005) |
| Beat (inter-ear difference) | ≤ ~30–35 Hz | Above this the two tones are heard as separate pitches, not a beat (Perrott & Nelson) |
| Channel delivery | Strict left/right separation (headphones) | The beat does not form if the tones mix acoustically before reaching the ears |
