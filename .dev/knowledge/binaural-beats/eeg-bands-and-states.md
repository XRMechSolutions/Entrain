# EEG Frequency Bands & Brain States

## The bands (Established baseline)

| Band | Hz range | Best-established association | Strength |
|------|----------|------------------------------|----------|
| Delta | ~0.5–4 | Deep NREM (slow-wave) sleep | Established (textbook) |
| Theta | ~4–8 | Drowsiness/sleep onset; hippocampal memory encoding; frontal-midline theta in working-memory load | Established for sleep/memory; looser for "creativity/meditation" |
| Alpha | ~8–13 | Relaxed wakefulness, eyes closed; "alpha blocking" (rises on eye closure, drops on opening) | Established (among EEG's most reproducible findings) |
| Beta | ~13–30 | Active, alert cognition; concentration; high beta linked to arousal/anxiety | Established for active waking; mixed for sub-band claims |
| Gamma | ~30–100+ (clusters ~40) | Feature "binding," cross-region integration, attentive perception | Mixed (influential but debated; artifact-prone band) |

Band boundaries are **conventions, not natural constants** — sources differ
(alpha 8–12 vs 8–13; gamma onset 25/30/35 Hz). The brain runs all bands at once;
ratios and cross-frequency coupling carry much of the information.

## The reverse-inference caveat (most important)

The band↔state literature is **correlational**: when a brain is in state Y, band X
tends to be prominent. It does **not** follow that externally *driving* an
oscillation at X *produces* state Y. That is a **reverse inference** — invalid
unless the band is specific to the state, which it usually isn't (theta appears in
both drowsiness *and* focused memory tasks).

This invalid leap — "alpha relates to calm, therefore a 10 Hz beat induces calm" —
is the load-bearing assumption of most binaural-beats marketing. A review of 184
resting-state psychiatric EEG studies found the *most common* result was **no
significant band difference**, and existing correlations were weak (~0.2–0.5) and
non-specific (Newson & Thiagarajan 2019). Whether driving a beat actually shifts
the EEG is tested in `does-entrainment-happen.md` — and it largely fails.
